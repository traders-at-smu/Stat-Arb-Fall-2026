REBUILDING SPY'S SHARE PRICE FROM ITS PARTS
Traders@SMU - Stat Arb - 20 Mar to 25 Sep 2026

HOW TO OPEN IT
--------------
1. Unzip this folder anywhere (Desktop is fine). Keep the files together -
   index.html reads the two .js files sitting next to it.
2. Double-click index.html. It opens in your default browser.
3. That's it. No install, no server, no internet connection needed.

If double-clicking opens a text editor instead of a browser, right-click
index.html -> Open With -> Chrome / Safari / Firefox.

Do NOT open index.html from inside the .zip preview - macOS and Windows both
show zip contents in a temporary folder where the .js files aren't reachable,
and the page will load blank. Unzip first.


WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING AT
----------------------
SPY's share price rebuilt two independent ways and compared against the real
thing: once from the 500 S&P 500 constituent equities, once from the eleven
Select Sector SPDR ETFs. Both are market-cap weighted with a divisor, the way
the index itself is computed.

Built on 2,680,494 ten-minute bars - 528 tickers, 5,109 buckets, 131 sessions.

Five tabs:

  Charts           Each series alone, then all three together with checkboxes.
                   The "Sampling" switch flips the charts between 10-minute
                   bars and daily closes.

  Intraday prices  Pick a session, see its 39 ten-minute buckets. Click any
                   figure in the sector column to break that bucket's price
                   into the eleven sectors behind it.

  Daily prices     All 126 sessions. Click any figure in the last two columns
                   to drill into the securities behind it, then click a sector
                   to go one level deeper, down to individual companies.

  Sector weights   How each sector's index weight moved across the window.

  Why the gap      The analysis: why eleven ETFs holding all 500 stocks do not
                   reproduce the index, and why that gap is not free money.


THE SHORT VERSION OF THE FINDING
--------------------------------
Rebuilt from the 500 equities, SPY comes back within $1.13.
Rebuilt from the eleven sector ETFs, it lands $4.39 short.

That shortfall is the Select Sector 24/50 capping rule - no company above 24%,
and everything above 4.8% scaled down if it collectively tops 50%. XLC is the
clearest case: it moves barely 60% as much as the Communication Services stocks
it holds, because Alphabet and Meta blow through the cap.

It is not arbitrageable. The funds are contractually required to hold weights
the index does not, so capturing the gap means single-stock overlays to undo
each cap - a real trade with financing and borrow costs.

Sampling ten times more often does not shrink the sector gap (tracking error
2.66% daily vs 2.68% intraday), which is how you know it is structural rather
than noise.
