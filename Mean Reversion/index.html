Rebuilding SPY's share price from its parts.

Both baskets are market-cap weighted with a divisor, the way the index itself is built, and both start at SPY's actual 20 March close of $648.57. Rebuilt from the 500 constituent equities, SPY comes back within 27 cents. Rebuilt from the eleven sector ETFs at those same index weights, it lands $2.92 short — and that shortfall is not a mistake or an arbitrage. It is the Select Sector capping rule, and it is measurable.

Why the sector ETFs don't add up to SPY

The obvious objection: if eleven funds contain all 500 stocks, holding them at index weights must reproduce the index — and any gap would be free money. The first half is right. The second isn't, and the reason is structural rather than a modelling error.

The weighting is provably correct

Regrouping the 500 equities into 11 sectors and cap-weighting the sectors' own returns reproduces the all-equity basket exactly — correlation 1.000000, tracking error 0.000%, maximum gap $0.00. That is an algebraic identity, and it rules out any error in the sector weights or the GICS mapping. Swap those underlying sector returns for the ETFs' actual returns and the level falls to $758.77. So the whole gap sits in one place: the ETFs do not return what their own sectors return.

Each ETF against the stocks it holds

S&P Dow Jones caps the Select Sector indices at 24/50 — no company above 24%, and if everything above 4.8% together exceeds 50% of the index, all of it is scaled down. Rebalanced quarterly. In S&P's own published example, Nvidia was cut to 4.5% of the technology index despite a far larger float weight. A capped fund cannot track an uncapped sector.

XLC is the clearest case at beta 0.60 — it moves barely half as much as the Communication Services stocks it holds, because Alphabet and Meta together blow through the 50% rule and get scaled back hard. Twelve of the fourteen largest ETF-versus-underlying divergences in the entire window are XLC days. XLY sits at 0.83 for the same reason; XLK runs the other way at 1.10, holding relatively more mid-cap tech once its giants are capped.

This is not arbitrageable. The gap isn't mispricing between the funds and their holdings — the funds are required to hold different weights than the index does. Capturing it would mean trading the sector basket against single-stock positions sized to undo each cap: a real trade with financing and borrow costs, not free money. The caps exist to satisfy regulated-investment-company diversification rules, so they are not going away.

Data notes

Spin-off correction. Honeywell spun off HONA on 29 June. In Compustat, HON's adjustment factor jumps 0.5 → 1.0 and its share count halves that day, making a naive adjusted-price chain read a −50.95% return. HON did not lose half its value — holders received HONA shares, and index methodology absorbs a spin-off through the divisor. That security-day is excluded; leaving it in was dragging the equity basket down about 90 cents. Every other move above 20% was checked: seven ordinary splits (APH, BKNG, CRWD, CVNA, DD, KLAC, MNST) are handled correctly at a maximum of 5.5%, and the rest are genuine news.

Residual tracking error. The equity basket's 1.53% tracking error against SPY is concentrated rather than systematic — three sessions drive most of it, and excluding them it falls to 0.90%. It reverses day to day (lag-1 autocorrelation −0.46) with cumulative drift of about −0.08%: the signature of isolated pricing noise, not a weighting bias. The S&P 500 weights by float-adjusted market cap while Compustat supplies total shares outstanding, so a small bias may remain, but the resulting top-10 concentration of 38.53% is in line with the index's own.

Membership and construction. Both baskets are market-cap weighted with a divisor and chained onto SPY's actual 20 March close of $648.57, on a price basis since SPY's quoted price excludes dividends. Membership is rebuilt every session, 504–507 names. Exact 2026 removal dates are not recoverable — Compustat's historical membership was withdrawn from WRDS and CRSP's ends 2025-12-31 — so the thirteen departing names are retired against the nine known addition dates; their combined weight is under 0.1%. Compustat's fromdate marks a constituent record's start, not the index join date: CCL, HON, XOM and BNY carry 2026 dates but were in CRSP's Dec-2025 index and are held from the window's start.
