Low-beta returns from ETF residuals: findings

Quant finance club · 31 ETF pairs · Alpaca SIP bars 2016-2026, tested on 2023-2026 · one tab per bar size · 2026-09-25

Can a low-beta return be built from the residual of an ETF against its parent (sector ETF against SPY, sub-industry ETF against its sector), using hedging and Kalman filters?

Method

  • Universe: SPY, the 11 sector SPDRs, 20 sub-industry sleeves; Alpaca SIP bars at 1, 5, 15 minutes and daily, 2016-2026; every test on 2023-2026 with nothing fitted after 2022.
  • Phase A (the beta half): hedge each child against its parent with a static OLS beta, a rolling OLS beta, or a Kalman filter with a per-session drift parameter, and measure what market beta is left, on the worst days too.
  • Phase B (the return half): test the hedged residual for autocorrelation, then trade it with mean reversion and momentum rules after a half-spread cost per leg.
  • Phase B2: choose entries, exits, stops and volatility or time-of-day filters from 735-784 settings per pair on 2016-2022, then score the chosen setting once on 2023-2026.
  • Pass bars: hedged beta inside ±0.10 (whole confidence interval), net Sharpe above 0.8 with strategy beta inside ±0.10.

Views

  • The beta half works at every bar size. A refreshed hedge removes market beta; a fixed one does not (sector betas moved 30-60% in four years). Intraday the Kalman filter beats any rolling window; at daily it ties.
  • The nesting in the figure is only half right. A sub-industry sleeve hedged against its sector alone keeps +0.1 to +0.5 of market beta; hedging against SPY and the sector together removes it and explains the most variance.
  • The return half fails at every bar size. Daily residuals are white noise. 5- and 15-minute residuals mean-revert, visibly and robustly, but the reversion is worth under one basis point per unit traded, below the cheapest half-spread. One-minute residuals are bid-ask bounce.
  • Tuning does not change it. Settings chosen in-sample decay to zero or below out of sample; even the best setting chosen on the test window itself cannot clear the bar intraday.
  • The per-pair winners are selection. With a 3.7-year test window a Sharpe ratio has a standard error near 0.5; the best of 12 settings on noise averages about +0.85. Read fixed-setting numbers, not best-of tables.

Keep

  • The Kalman hedge with per-session drift (δ = 1e-3 daily, 1e-4 intraday): every sector inside ±0.02 intraday, every sleeve inside ±0.025 with both hedges, tails included.
  • Hedge sleeves against SPY and the sector together, never the sector alone.
  • The equivalence test for beta (whole confidence interval inside ±0.10): with 20,000+ bars a “covers zero” test fails betas that are economically nothing.
  • The breakeven-cost framing: report what the signal is worth per unit traded next to what the pair costs to trade.
  • The intraday reversion itself: real, robust across pairs and years, and the natural input to a market-making or execution study rather than a taker strategy.
  • The data set and pipeline: 32 symbols, four bar sizes, 2016-2026, adjusted and session-aligned, reusable for Tier 3 stocks.

Throw away

  • Static hedge ratios of any kind.
  • Sector-only hedges for sub-industry sleeves.
  • Spread z-score trades on the daily residual: half-lives of 80-180 days and no stationarity anywhere.
  • Momentum on intraday residuals: negative at every setting.
  • Thin sleeves intraday (XPH, HACK, IAI, GDX): 10-25% of variance explained, 3 bp half-spreads, nothing to trade.
  • Anything at 1 minute: breakeven 0.35 bp against costs no ETF meets.
  • Best-of-N per-pair tables as evidence.

If it continues

  • A maker-execution study at 5-15 minutes: the reversion is worth 0.6-1.5 bp per unit; passive fills with rebates are the only way to collect it, and that changes the fill model.
  • A second test window (2020-2022) for the hedge results, which is where a tail-beta failure would show up.
  • Non-price signals on the beta-free daily residual (flows, earnings, rebalancing), since price history alone carries nothing at daily.
  • Tier 3: single stocks against their sector ETF, where residuals are larger and half-lives shorter.

Daily bars

  • Static betas from 2016-22 leave −0.2 to −0.6 of market beta in 2023-26; a 120-day rolling or 32-day Kalman hedge leaves ±0.07 at most, and holds on the worst days.
  • Hedging removes about half of a sector's variance (XLK 0.80, XLY 0.72) and far less for defensives (XLU 0.11, XLP 0.10).
  • The residual is white noise: lag-1 autocorrelation −0.004, no significant Ljung-Box statistic on any sector.
  • Fixed strategy settings earn a net Sharpe of about 0.3; settings chosen in-sample (0.76) score 0.09 out of sample. Costs do not matter here; the absence of signal does.

15-min bars

  • A Kalman hedge with a 20-day memory leaves every sector inside ±0.014 and every sleeve inside ±0.024; the rolling window is second on every metric.
  • The residual mean-reverts: variance ratio 0.72 at 10 bars, Ljung-Box significant on a third of sectors and half of sleeves.
  • Gross Sharpe reaches 0.6-0.8 on half the pairs; net of one half-spread per leg it is negative on every fixed setting, because the reversion is worth 0.6-1.5 bp per unit traded.
  • In-sample tuning (784 settings) picks extreme entries, partial exits and a high-volatility gate; those choices score −0.45 out of sample. Ceiling on the test window itself: 0.6.

5-min bars

  • An 11-day-memory Kalman hedge leaves every sector inside ±0.012 and every sleeve inside ±0.016; the rolling window leaves up to 0.08 on thin sleeves.
  • Strongest reversion of any bar size: Ljung-Box significant on 7 of 11 sectors and all 20 sleeves; gross Sharpe above 0.8 on 17 of 31 pairs at their best setting.
  • Breakeven is 0.6-0.9 bp per unit traded, under SPY's own half-spread; every net number is negative (median −0.78 at the best setting per pair).
  • In-sample tuning: 0.30 in, −0.23 out, none above 0.8. Thin sleeves are microstructure noise around a correct hedge.

1-min bars

  • The cleanest hedge of all: a 2-5 day-memory Kalman leaves every sector inside ±0.006 on 360,000 test bars per pair.
  • Less is left to trade: variance removed falls to 0.45 for sectors and near zero for thin sleeves as asynchronous trading lowers measured co-movement.
  • Lag-1 autocorrelation is negative on 31 of 31 pairs and gone by lag 2: bid-ask bounce, not signal. Breakeven 0.35 bp against 1-4 bp costs.
  • Fixed settings: gross 0.3, net −0.7 (sectors) to −5 (sleeves). In-sample tuning (784 settings): 0.25 in, −0.34 out, ceiling on the test window 0.42, the lowest of any bar size.
