Daily bars: findings

Out-of-sample window 2023-01-01 to 2026-09-22, 933 bars per pair on the SPY clock. Signals use bars up to t−1; costs are a half-spread per leg on every position change.

  • The daily residual is white noise. Median lag-1 autocorrelation is −0.004 for sectors and −0.024 for sleeves; no sector has a significant Ljung-Box statistic and only 4 of 20 sleeves do; variance ratios at 2, 5 and 10 days are 0.93-0.99. Nothing to time.
  • No fixed strategy setting clears the bar. The best single setting across sectors is mean reversion at |z| > 2.5 with a median net Sharpe of 0.29 and 2 of 11 sectors above 0.8; across sleeves it is 60-day momentum at 0.27 with 4 of 20. Median net returns are 2-4% a year with 13-25% drawdowns. Costs are irrelevant here: breakeven is 90-150 bp against a 1-4 bp cost, because positions last 40-100 days.
  • The per-pair table looks better and should not be believed. Picking each pair's best of 12 settings gives a median net Sharpe of 0.70 and 12 of 31 pairs above 0.8. With a 3.7-year test window the standard error of a Sharpe ratio is about 0.5, so the best of 12 draws on a true-zero residual lands near +0.85 on average. The table is consistent with noise.
  • Every strategy is beta-neutral (median strategy beta to SPY between −0.02 and +0.02, 90-100% of pairs inside ±0.10): the hedge does its job, the signal does not.

The original question, Daily bars

NOCan we obtain a low-beta return from the ETF residual at this bar size?

The residual can be made beta-free (Phase A) but nothing in it pays with these signals: a market-neutral book that earns 2-4% a year at Sharpe 0.3 is noise around zero. A different signal (flows, fundamentals, events) would be needed to turn the daily residual into a return. A 735-setting in-sample search (Phase B2) does not change this: settings chosen on 2016-2022 score a median net Sharpe of 0.09 on 2023-2026.

Phase A, the beta half: YES. Phase B, the return half: NO, the daily residual is statistically white noise. Lag-1 autocorrelation is −0.004 for sectors, no sector and only 4 of 20 sleeves show a significant Ljung-Box statistic, and variance ratios sit at 0.93-0.99. The best fixed strategy setting earns a median net Sharpe of 0.29 with 2 of 11 sectors above 0.8; the per-pair best-of-12 reaches 0.70, which is what 12 tries on noise produce when the Sharpe standard error is about 0.5.

The original question, 15-min bars

NOCan we obtain a low-beta return from the ETF residual at this bar size?

The residual is beta-free and mean-reverting at 15 minutes, but the reversion is smaller than the spread. Only sub-basis-point execution (a maker, not a taker) turns this into a return; at taker costs the answer is no. A 784-setting in-sample search confirms it: in-sample winners score −0.45 out of sample, and even the best setting picked on the test window itself reaches only 0.6. One outlier, XLE 60-bar momentum at net Sharpe 1.36, is one of 372 pair-setting combinations and is a Phase C candidate, not a result.

Phase A, the beta half: YES. Phase B, the return half: NO, after costs, although the signal is real. The 15-minute residual mean-reverts (variance ratio 0.72 at 10 bars, Ljung-Box significant on 36% of sectors and 45-65% of sleeves), and gross Sharpe ratios reach a median of 0.63 for the best sector setting and 0.75 for the best setting per pair. The original question, 5-min bars

NOCan we obtain a low-beta return from the ETF residual at this bar size?

Beta-free and clearly mean-reverting, but the 5-minute reversion is worth under one basis point per unit traded, which is less than the half-spread of even SPY. It is a market-maker's edge, not a taker's. Thin sleeves (XPH, HACK, IAI, GDX) add nothing: their residual is microstructure noise around a good hedge. The in-sample search makes the same point: median out-of-sample net Sharpe −0.23 for the in-sample winners, none above 0.8.

The original question, 1-min bars

NOCan we obtain a low-beta return from the ETF residual at this bar size?

At one minute the hedge is as clean as it gets (Phase A) and the residual is pure microstructure. Nothing survives even a fraction of the spread. A 784-setting in-sample search agrees: in-sample winners score −0.34 out of sample, and the ceiling on the test window itself is 0.42.

Phase A, the beta half: YES. Phase B, the return half: NO, and by the widest margin. The 1-minute residual has a negative lag-1 autocorrelation on 31 of 31 pairs (−0.02 for sectors, −0.10 for sleeves), which is bid-ask bounce, not a tradable signal. The best fixed setting earns 0.34-0.36 gross and −0.7 to −5.2 net; breakeven is 0.35 bp per unit traded against 1-4 bp costs, with 120-370 round trips a year for mean reversion and over 1,000 for momentum.

NODoes the hedged residual pay after costs at this bar size?

Method

  • Residuals are the Phase A hedged positions: Tier 1 sectors hedged against SPY, Tier 2 sleeves hedged against SPY and their sector (3-state filter) and, for comparison, against SPY alone. Kalman δ = 1e-3 per session at daily, 1e-4 intraday, filtered (one-step-ahead) betas only. NaN bars (overnight, missing prints) break every position: nothing is earned across a break and the position is re-opened flat.
  • Diagnostics on the out-of-sample residual: autocorrelation at lags 1-20 with a 2-standard-error band, Ljung-Box Q at lag 10, Lo-MacKinlay variance ratios at 2, 5 and 10 bars (VR < 1 mean reversion, > 1 trending, ≈ 1 random walk), AR(1) half-life of the cumulative residual.
  • Mean reversion: z = (cumulative residual − trailing mean) / trailing std over the Phase A middle window (120 days daily, 3 sessions intraday); enter when |z| exceeds 1.5, 2 or 2.5, exit when z crosses 0, stop beyond |z| = 4. Momentum: sign of the trailing k-bar residual sum, held h bars, k and h in {5, 20, 60}. Signals use bars up to t−1 and are applied to bar t.
  • Costs: a half-spread per leg per unit of turnover (SPY and the large sectors 0.5 bp, XLC/XLRE 0.75-1 bp, liquid sleeves 1 bp, thin sleeves 3 bp), so a round trip pays twice the pair cost. Breakeven bps is the cost at which net Sharpe would be zero; compare it with the pair's cost column. Sharpe is annualized with 252 × bars-per-day bars per year.
  • Refined settings (Phase B2): the grid is z-window {10, 20, 60, 120, 250 days} at daily or {1, 3, 5, 10 sessions} intraday, entry {1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0}, exit at {0, 0.5, 1.0} (partial reversion), stop {3, 4, none}, momentum k {2, 5, 10, 20, 60} (intraday {5, 20, 60, 2 sessions}) and hold {1, 5, 20, 60}, each with an entry filter: none, trailing residual volatility above its expanding median (volhi), below it (vollo), or intraday only 10:00-15:30 ET (tod). Vol uses a 20-day (daily) or 5-session (intraday) window and the median is computed through t−1. A setting needs 20 (daily) or 50 (intraday) in-sample trades to be eligible. Selection uses in-sample net Sharpe only; the out-of-sample columns are computed once, after selection.
  • Pass bars from the plan: net Sharpe above 0.8 and realized beta of the strategy P&L to SPY inside ±0.10. The strategy grid uses one parameter set for every pair (no selection); the per-pair table shows each pair's best of the 12 parameter sets and therefore carries selection bias, which the verdicts discount. With a 3.7-year test window the standard error of an annualized Sharpe ratio is about 0.5, so the best of 12 settings on a true-zero residual averages about +0.85.
